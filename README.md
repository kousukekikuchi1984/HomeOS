# Home OS Charter

## Preamble

- A home is a long-lived social asset, operated across decades rather than merely built and finished.
- High-tech layers are short-lived and can make homes fragile when they become embedded too deeply.
- Home OS does not exist to make homes high-tech. It exists to protect low-tech homes from high-tech dependencies.

## Part I: The Home Comes First

The home must remain sovereign over the systems that support it.

### Article 1: House First

Essential housing functions must not depend on Home OS availability.

### Article 2: Low-tech Core

Core housing performance must be achieved through physical design, construction quality, and durable materials.

### Article 3: Replaceable High-tech

Short-lived technologies must remain replaceable supporting layers, never structural dependencies.

### Article 4: Graceful Degradation

When systems fail, convenience must fail before habitability.

## Part II: The Home Must Be Operable Across Its Lifecycle

A home must remain observable, maintainable, and accountable throughout its life.

### Article 5: Observability by Design

Homes must be designed so that deterioration, failure, and performance loss can be detected before they become hidden liabilities.

### Article 6: Maintainability by Design

Inspection, replacement, and repair must be possible without unnecessary destruction of the building itself.

### Article 7: Asset Accountability

The condition, history, and future update plans of a home must remain explainable and traceable.

## Part III: The Home Must Connect Without Being Captured

A home may connect to external systems, but must not be captured by them.

### Article 8: Local First

Critical control and critical data must function locally whenever possible.

### Article 9: Privacy First, City Ready

Home OS may support City OS through anonymized and aggregated outputs, but raw household data must not become the default basis of social infrastructure.

### Article 10: Open Interoperability

Home equipment and services must be managed through durable abstract interfaces that resist vendor lock-in and short-lived standards.

## Closing Statement

Home OS is not a system for making homes dependent on technology.

It is a system for ensuring that technology remains subordinate to the long life of the home.
