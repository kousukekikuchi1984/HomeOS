# Home OS Charter

## Preamble
We believe a home is not something that is merely built and finished, but a long-lived social asset operated over decades.

Home OS is foundational infrastructure that integrates in-home energy, equipment, environmental conditions, deterioration signals, and maintenance history to protect comfort and safety while maximizing long-term housing asset value.

We reject the idea that technology progress should make homes fragile. We therefore declare this principle:

**Home OS exists to prevent low-tech homes from being broken by high-tech layers.**

## Article 1: House First
A home must retain essential function even if Home OS is unavailable.
Water supply, drainage, ventilation, daylight, manual control, and structural safety must not depend on OS uptime.

## Article 2: Low-tech Core
Core housing performance—structure, waterproofing, insulation, passive airflow, and drainage—must be achieved through physical design and construction quality.

## Article 3: Replaceable High-tech
Sensors, connectivity, AI, cloud services, and user interfaces must be implemented as replaceable supporting layers.
Short-life technologies must never hold long-life housing assets hostage.

## Article 4: Observability by Design
Homes should be designed from the start for observation, diagnosis, inspection, and repair.
A home must provide observability that prevents hidden deterioration from being ignored.

## Article 5: Maintainability by Design
Inspection, replacement, and updates must be possible with minimal damage to the building itself.
Designs that cannot be maintained become liabilities for future generations.

## Article 6: Asset Accountability
Home condition, history, and update plans should remain explainable and traceable.
Home OS supports accountability to residents, operators, and future owners.

## Article 7: Local First
Critical control and critical data should function locally whenever possible.
Cloud services are extensions, not prerequisites.

## Article 8: Privacy First, City Ready
Home OS optimizes each home locally, and shares only anonymized, aggregated outputs with City OS.
Raw household data must not be the default basis of social infrastructure.

## Article 9: Open Interoperability
Home equipment should avoid lock-in to specific vendors or short-lived standards.
It should be managed through durable abstract interfaces.

## Article 10: Graceful Degradation
When failures occur, convenience features should degrade first while life-supporting functions remain last.
The standard is not "everything works or nothing works," but "minimum living function is always preserved."

## Closing Statement
Home OS does not prescribe architectural style.
It enables a home's full lifecycle to remain observable, diagnosable, and updatable.

And above all:

**Home OS is an OS that keeps low-tech homes from being damaged by high-tech dependencies.**
